The idea

One product, many spaces, each shaped by a kit

The code knows only eight things: a space, an admin, a publisher, a learner, a group, a subject, a chapter and a lesson. A kit decides what people see: the language and its direction, the logo and colours, and the name of each of those eight things. Change the kit and every word on every page changes. No developer, no new code.

Try it: the same space, three kits, two languages

      Who does what

      Four roles in the product, and a chain of appointment

      Owner (you)Creates spaces, picks and edits kits, appoints each space's admin with an end date. Sees kits, admins and usage totals. Never sees inside a space.
      AdminRuns one space: appoints publishers and learners with end dates, forms groups, creates subjects and assigns them. Can reset the passwords of people they appointed.
      PublisherTeacher, trainer or lecturer. Builds their subjects out of chapters, lessons and materials: PDF, image, video link, web link, note.
      LearnerStudent, employee or trainee. Sees exactly the subjects assigned to them or to their group, and nothing else.
      The promise

      One sentence that release 1 must make true

      A non-technical owner shapes one organization's learning space (its name, language, theme and vocabulary), appoints its admin, and that organization's publishers share chapters, lessons and materials with exactly the learners assigned to them, from beginning to end, at zero hosting cost.

      Not in this release: grading, quizzes, certificates, messaging, attendance, video hosting, email, billing, gamification, more than one owner. Each is listed in Later with the trigger that brings it back.

      How the work travels

      The same roles as the platform, one line, no extra approval layers

      This is the operating model adopted in the platform repository on 15 October 2026, in its short form: Product Manager writes the contract, one critic reviews it once, the Lead owns completion, the Builder writes the failing test before the code, an Independent Verifier from another AI vendor gives one PASS or HOLD, and your merge is the acceptance. What this repository does not carry over is the platform's own machinery: Paperclip packages, the Publisher watcher and the laptop scripts.

      Roadmap

      Why release 1 is small, and when each idea arrives

      Parked is not rejected. Every idea you raised is written down with a one-sentence promise, the tables it adds, the decision it needs, and the event that starts it. The order is forced by four things.

      Each idea stands on release 1Guardians need learners to watch. Exam results need subjects, groups and learners. Print orders need materials and guardians. Automatic WhatsApp codes need registered numbers. Nothing above the floor can be built, or tested, before the floor exists.
      Done must stay a yes-or-no questionFive tests you can check in one sitting. With the four ideas inside it would be fifteen or more, and "finished" would drift. Your own manifesto: narrow and complete beats broad and partial.
      Each parked idea needs a decision only you can takeMoney and a Meta account, a contract with a print shop and its access to files, whether a parent may see that a child opened a lesson. Bundling them would stop release 1 on decisions unrelated to sharing lessons.
      Small releases arrive, and teachThe brainstorm rejected Moodle and Open edX for carrying everything at once. Release 1 is the opposite: the smallest space Mesk School can use next week. Release 2 starts from real teachers' feedback, which is worth more than any plan.
